Orlok's ledger — DM notes

🟡 Not canon yet. This is iPad item 4, so the players open this page: the DM tab asks before it shows these notes. Print the crate tags on cardstock. Built 3 Nov for Scott's review. Scott chose the design (ledger + crate tags, one patient unaccounted for). Every patient past the fox and the owl, the numbers, the dates, the doe and where she is are this prop's invention, logged in CANON.md.

The set-up

  • The cellar (stair down from the kitchen): straw-lined crates along the walls, a lamp, and the ledger open on a lectern by the stair. The read-aloud from the run sheet lands here: a fox in a splint, an owl with a wrapped wing. It isn't a slaughterhouse. It's a clinic.
  • Six crates carry tags (print: crate tags, cardstock). Hand a tag over each time someone checks a crate, or all six at once if the table is slow.
  • Vinny's liquor crates are stacked against the back (east) wall, forty years of dust on them. One stack is a door: it swings on a hinged pallet into Vinny's old raid room, dug under the ballroom floor, where he hid the good stock when the law came up the hill. Orlok made it his infirmary.
  • The dates read ult. (last month) and inst. (this month), the way a nineteenth-century hand keeps books. Nothing in the puzzle turns on them.

The solution (about ten minutes)

Eight patients in the book. Three are released. Five are still in care, and only four of them are in a crate.

Her entry says she's kept quiet in the old room, and no blood has been taken because she wouldn't survive it. Once they know they're looking for a deer, the trail is plain:

  • Hoof-scuffs and drag marks in the dust run to Vinny's stack. Investigation or Perception DC 10 once they know what to look for; DC 18 if they're searching blind.
  • A lamp hook on the stack with fresh drips of wax, and a smear of salve on the pallet edge.
  • The stack swings. Behind it: the infirmary.

Russ's shortcut

Speak with Animals skips the ledger. The patients say it outright: they're fed, mended, and they let him take a little because he asks. Ask any of them who's missing and they'll tell Russ.

Fox: “The deer. He took her through the bottles. She smells bad. Not dead-bad. Hot-bad.”

Opossum: “He sits with her. All day. Then he sits with us.”

Owl, unimpressed: “He reads to the crow. The crow doesn't deserve it.”

Fail-forward

If they stall, push one rung at a time:

  1. ~5 min: a hoof knocks against wood somewhere behind the bottles. Then a long, wet breath.
  2. ~8 min: any check on the ledger: point at the one entry with no release and no crate, and at “the old room.”
  3. Still stuck: Orlok on the stair, without looking at them: “She's through there. Mind the stitches. Don't let the lamp near her eyes.” It costs the scene some magic and none of the reveal.

In the infirmary — badge requirement 2

  • A table scrubbed white, bandages, a mortar and pestle, a chair pulled close to a straw bed. The doe is on it: eleven stitches, fevered, too weak to stand. She's the worst-off patient Russ dresses.
  • Pots of Sun's-mercy seedlings, dead white, all leaning toward a light-well grate high in the wall that opens under the terrace. Orlok can't stand in its light, so the pots sit just out of it.
  • Tend your elder's garden: Russ moves them into the light from the grate, or channels it (Nature / Plant Growth DC 14, or a spell slot, as on the run sheet). They open gold. He dresses the doe's wound with it.
  • Orlok watches a child do in an afternoon what beat him for a century. He still won't sign: refusal 2 of 3.

Seams for Scott

  • Daylight at night. In the fiction the house runs 7PM to 3AM, so the grate may hold moonlight, not sun. If so, the channelled version is the only one that works.
  • The run sheet's cue 12 says the puzzle releases a key and puts the fox and owl in the hidden room. This prop puts the tagged crates in the outer cellar and the hidden room behind Vinny's crates, with no key.
  • The raccoon's burns come from the new wire along the east wall: a quiet nod to the hot-wired power. Cut the line if it says too much.
