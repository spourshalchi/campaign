## The spine, if you want one

**Everyone in this house wants revenge except the monsters.**

The party arrives to punish something. The Gjenganger wants revenge on people a century dead. The
Spirit wants revenge and it's the only righteous version in the building. Gnomey has carried a

## A. Gnomey — filling in "one Halloween some spooky shit went down"

**A1. The dare is the origin of everything. (Goals 1, 4)**
The crew dared each other to ring the doorbell. Gnomey was smallest, so Gnomey got sent. Somebody
*answered*. That's the night Poe Boy learned there was something in that house — and Sesh 7 has him
going back alone for samples. Which means the dead kid, the soul cage, tonight's party and the invite
in his locker all trace back to a dare Gnomey took because the crew underestimated him.
He built his own nemesis. It's Angelique's guilt in a different key, and it's his *own* stated origin
— revenge-prone because always underestimated — turned on him.

**A2. What actually happened at the door. (Goals 3 → 4, one object)**
His version, told for years: he got chased. The house's version, found inside: they opened the door,
looked at a gnome the size of a mailbox, and **did nothing**. Somewhere in the front hall is proof —
see A3. First pass it reads as *they've been watching us since we were children*. Second pass it's the
earliest evidence in the building that these three have never hurt anyone.

**A3. The guest book. (Goals 3, 4)**
Names scratched into the plaster by the front door, decades of them, dated. Gnomey's is there in his
own middle-school handwriting. Reads as a kill list. It's a **guest book** — the vampires write down
everyone who ever came to the door, because almost nobody ever does. Orlok keeps it. Dracula is
offended that most entries are children.

**A4. The rock, kept. (Goal 4)**
They threw rocks at the windows. One is still inside, on a shelf, in the parlour, with the date on a
card under it like a museum label. Horrible, then sad. Pairs with a window that has been mended from
the inside with whatever was to hand.

**A5. The ledger, and the skim that never stopped. (Goals 1, 2, 4 — strongest for his arc)**
Gnomey kept a book on the silly banz money, mob-boss style, which is how he caught the skim. In Poe
Boy's setup room there is **another ledger in the same hand**: scares logged, priced, sorted by quality.
And the vampires appear in it as a cost line. He's skimming *them* — that's Sesh 5's "fed up with him"
made into a document instead of a mood. Gnomey is the only person alive who can read that page and know
exactly what he's looking at, because it was done to him for silly banz when he was eleven.
One line item has no name, just a date and a grade. That's the dead kid.

**A6. The single silly band. (Goal 4, one prop)**
One silly band, years old, in Poe Boy's stuff in the mansion. He kept his cut. Gnomey's entire
grievance, confirmed, in an object worth nothing.

**A7. The rumor is his revenge, and it's why the vampires are alive. (Goals 2, 4)**
After the falling out he did what he does — took an extravagant petty revenge. He seeded the story that
the house was haunted and something in it ate a kid, to wreck the crew's nerve and take their spot.
It worked far better than intended: the town's fear of that house is substantially **his**, and that
fear is the only reason three vampires got left alone for a generation. If the vampires ever work out
who did it, Dracula wants to shake his hand. Also: it's what made the house perfect for Poe Boy.

**A8. He is the map. (Goal 1, mechanical)**
Doc-true that he knows back roads and pumpkin patches. He's the one who knows the grounds — the way in
that isn't the boarded front door, the patch behind the house, where the ground is soft. If the coffins
or the family plot matter (Sesh 2's staking method), he's the reason the party can find them without
being told.

**A9. The cigarettes. (Goals 1, 4)**
His bathroom trade has a leak: someone's been buying volume and reselling. It's Lil Stinky, taking a
cut, running them up the hill. The vampires don't smoke. They're for Angelique, who has been in that
house more nights than anyone knows — which is Sesh 5's "she wants to warn them but can't" with a
paper trail. *Or* they're what Poe Boy pays the vampires with, which is funnier and meaner.

**A10. Blood tie — contested, flagging it. (Goal 1)**
Scott floated in doc comments that one PC could be tied to the mansion by blood, with the library as
the place to hint it. It currently sits with John on the checkout card, but that card is 🟡 anyway.
Gnomey's version: the wholesome family has exactly one ancestor nobody discusses, and he's in the
house's paper. His mob-boss idolization stops being a bit and becomes inheritance. **Do not run this
and John's at the same time** — pick one.

## B. Simone — building a connection from nothing

**B1. She sold Poe Boy the working part. (Goals 3, 4 — the heaviest option)**
Sesh 7 is explicit that the soul cage was "cast badly, with no vessel prepared" by someone who had only
been *reading* about the occult. So why did it catch anything at all? Because the component wasn't his.
Months earlier, a customer paid Simone for a spell. She has the receipt. She sells revenge as a service
and one of her products is the reason a kid is in a computer.
This is the single best inversion available for her: the party spends the night being told a monster
did this, and her own ledger says otherwise.
*Softer variant if that's too much weight:* it wasn't a spell, it was **access** — she let him copy out
of her books during detention.

**B2. Detention is the tie. (Goals 1, 2)**
Doc-true: she's in detention constantly for casting on school grounds. She is therefore the one PC who
has spent hours in a room alone with Poe Boy, who "studies the occult" (Sesh 3). He was charming. He
asked good questions. He took notes. She was flattered — nobody else at that school treats what she does
as real. She taught him, and she'll spend tonight working out how much.

**B3. The love spell was the bait. (Goal 4)**
Sesh 7: the dead kid was lured by a fake AIM screen name flirting with him, and Angelique typed some of
it. Simone's other product line is **love spells on crushes**. Someone commissioned one against the kid
— paid for, logged, delivered. When the party reaches the Spirit, he doesn't know Simone. But she knows
the name in her book.

**B4. Her patron's mark is already on the house. (Goals 3 → 4)**
The upside-down red cross is somewhere in the mansion, carved old, long before her. Reads as *my patron
claimed this place, I was always meant to come here*. Inverts: it isn't a claim, it's a **ward** — the
same shape the devil-banisher used, cut by people who were keeping something in. She's been wearing the
seal of the exterminators.

**B5. The patron gets loud inside. (Goals 1, 3, 5)**
Her powers come in clearer in the house — the goat is *interested*. Reads as confirmation the place is
evil. It's actually interested in exactly one thing: a soul that has been bound wrong, sitting in a
machine upstairs, ownerless. Give her a real choice at the end — her patron would very much like to be
handed that kid, and it would pay. That's her revenge theme with the knife pointed the right way.
Hooks the corridor fight too, if the Gjenganger stays: an improperly buried soul is the same problem.

**B6. The torn page. (Goal 4, one prop)**
The ritual she recognizes is from her own book, and the page is gone. It's in the mansion, in a room
only Poe Boy used, with her marginalia still on it in her handwriting.

**B7. The band is the leash. (Goal 1)**
Doc-true that she's saving for a show in town at the end of the year. Poe Boy is a DJ with real
underground connections (Sesh 3) — he's on that bill, or he can get her on the list, or he's holding
her money. A reason to go in that isn't the class project, and a reason she's slow to turn on him.
Pays off at the collapse: after the recording plays, she gets her show and he doesn't.

**B8. Simone and Gnomey are the two who came for the money. (Goal 1)**
He's a hustler who loves money; she's saving for tickets. Whatever's worth taking in that house — the
developer's cash, Poe Boy's advance, the vampires' actual antiques — those two are the ones who go
looking in rooms nobody sent them to. That's your "reason to explore" for both of them and it needs no
new lore at all.

**B9. Aggressively normal, and that's the wound. (Goal 1, quiet)**
Two atheist parents who think it's a phase. Everyone else in that house has a dead parent, a stolen
life, a century of loneliness, a grudge. She has a perfectly nice family in a perfectly nice house and
no reason to be here. The vampires — who are a **found family squatting in a house together** — are the
first thing she's ever met that takes her seriously without being scared of her. That makes her the
hardest to convince they're bad, and the most useful when the party is wrong.

## C. The braid — one reveal that uses both

**C1. Gnomey bought a revenge spell from Simone. (Goals 1, 3, 4)**
He is doc-true petty, extravagant, and holding a grudge over the silly banz. She doc-true sells revenge
spells to kids with beef. He paid her. Neither has mentioned it, because he doesn't want to admit it
didn't work and she doesn't discuss clients.
Then: something in the house is going after Poe Boy's people, or is coming when his name is spoken, and
it is answering to a spell with both their fingerprints on it. Reads as *the house is hunting*. Inverts
to *we made this, three years ago, over friendship bracelets.*
It also solves Scott's open question #8 — **why these five got the invite.** Poe Boy's guest list is
the people who have wanted him hurt. He thinks fear tastes better off someone who hates you.

**C2. Three ledgers. (Goal 4, structural)**
Gnomey's book, Simone's book, Poe Boy's book. Three kids in one town running businesses on other
people's feelings. Two of them price things in dollars. The third has a line with no name on it.
Put all three on the table in the same scene and you have the whole thesis without a speech.

**C3. Division of labor for the reveals. (Goals 1, 4)**
Everything that looks like vampire malice has a receipt with a human name on it, and each PC is
equipped to find a different receipt: **Gnomey finds the money.** **Simone finds the magic.**
**Bix finds the family** (per Sesh 8, Edward). Nobody can finish the inversion alone.

## 1. The backstory

- **Simone's first-ever customer was the kid who becomes the Spirit.** She was twelve or thirteen.
  The timeline works: Sesh 8 has the backpack in that house since before some of these kids started
  high school.
- **His wish was "I want Angelique to notice me."** Not love. *Notice.*
- **She overcooked it.** First sale, she wanted it to work, and she had no idea what she was doing.
- **It was granted precisely.** He became visible — to the crew, to the popular kids. Being noticed by
  those people isn't a gift, it's a target. Angelique noticed him and played along with the bullying,
  because that's what you do at that lunch table. He asked to stop being invisible, and invisible was
  the only thing keeping him alive.
- **The spell recruited its own bait.** Poe Boy needed someone who could write convincingly to *this
  specific kid.* The only person qualified was the girl who'd been noticing him for months. That's why
  Angelique got handed the keyboard. The spell built the murder weapon and gave it to the guy shopping
  for one.
- **It also made him certain.** He couldn't doubt her. Every ambiguous message read as yes. So the dare
  worked, he wasn't frightened enough to leave, and he kept typing after the door shut because he was
  sure she was coming.
- **Nobody ever cancelled it.** It's still running.
- **She scared herself.** Everything she's sold since is theater she knows is theater. That's why her
  business is 90% placebo — a detail the party can work out about her.

## 2. What it did to Angelique

- **She still notices.** The compulsion is intact and its object is gone, so it reads as an obsession
  with an absence: the empty desk, the locker nobody reassigned, the fact that no one else notices.
- **She's pulled toward the house,** because that's where he is. Which quietly explains something the
  doc needs — she knows that building well enough to find the party first and tell them to hide
  (Sesh 5). She's been coming back for years without being able to say why.
- **She doesn't know she's enchanted.** She thinks it's guilt. It's both.
- **⚠️ The rule that keeps her culpable: the spell only ever made her notice.** It didn't make her
  cruel, didn't make her type, didn't make her come back. Everything she *did* with the noticing was
  hers. She doesn't get "a spell made me do it" — she gets the worse version, where she was made to
  look and chose all of it after that.

## 3. In the mansion

- **In the backpack, alongside the doc's name-in-Sharpie:** something Simone made — a knotted string, a
  folded paper, a jar. **She recognizes her own work.** Nobody else in the room knows what they're
  looking at, and she can say nothing.
- **He kept it for years,** in a bag, in that house, because he still thought it might be working.
- **Also in the bag: a patch or a warped tape of her band.** He was a fan. He'd have been in that crowd.
- **She can never prove any of it.** A botched love spell's symptoms are indistinguishable from being
  fourteen and lonely. There's no test and no one to ask. She isn't a murderer or an accomplice —
  she's someone with a *possible* debt and no way to settle it.
- **The spell is the only intact thing left in him.** His name is gone, his face is gone. What survived
  five years in a machine is the magic, because magic doesn't rot the way a person does.

## 4. Why only Simone can move him

- **The love spell is her thread, and it's the only handle on that boy's soul in existence.** She tied
  it at thirteen and never cut it.
- **The goat can lend power but can't act.** It has no grip on him. Nobody does except her.
- Her mistake is the only lifeline. She isn't a conduit — she's the only possible surgeon, because it's
  her stitch.

## 5. The ask

- **Opener, comic:** he wants his twenty bucks back. Funny for two seconds, and it's the setup for the
  price being every dollar she has.
- **Optional rejected ask:** *"Make her love me."* She says no. He arrives at the real one himself,
  which shows he grew up in there.
- **The real ask: take me to the show.** He's been in a box for five years and the one thing he wants is
  the exact thing she's been saving for.
- **Mechanically:** rebind him from the machine to the thumb drive that's already in his backpack.
  He rides to the booth in a pocket and is *present* at his own exposure instead of being a file played
  about him.

## 6. The price

- **The goat appears, can grant it, names a price, and will not say what the price is for.**
- **The rule: it asks before it explains.** If she knows what she's buying it's shopping. Paying without
  knowing is what makes it a faith test — and it means she doesn't need to already care about the kid.
  The caring becomes retroactive.
- **The price is all of her concert savings**, in a **goat-shaped piggy bank**. She's been feeding coins
  to a goat for years; she built the altar before she knew it was an altar, and her patron's price is
  literally itself.
- **It's in her bag because she was going to buy the ticket that day.** She brought it into town to turn
  into cash.
- **She breaks it on the floor and counts out coins.** It should take an embarrassing amount of time and
  the whole party should have to stand there.
- **She does it alone.** No party vote, no witnesses. Faith with an audience is a performance, and
  performance is what she's growing out of.
- **The goat will not haggle** — the first time it has ever refused to, and that silence does more than
  a threat. **It refuses a group buy.** If Gnomey offers to cover it, no. It has to be hers, and it has
  to be the concert.

## 7. ⛔ The anti-gating rule — do not violate this

**The computer information is never on the table.** The AIM chat, the backpack, his name,
`scream_test_03_GOOD.wav`, the USB — all unconditional, all delivered by scene structure, because
Sesh 8 has him hand it over on his own: *his ask isn't directions, it's expose him.*

What Simone is buying is **only whether the boy himself is preserved.** The party can copy the file and
win the entire night without ever caring what happens to him — that's the default path and it works.

This is also the theme: **Poe Boy turned a person into a media file, and the party gets to decide
whether anyone in that house treats him as a person instead of evidence.**

**The no-path, designed to be good and not a punishment:**
- They still win. Recording plays, glamour breaks, Volvo, grounded, boos. Identical finale.
- At sunrise the machine just goes dark. No goodbye, no name in the epilogue, nobody thanks him.
- Optional: the goat takes him later anyway, for free, because an ownerless soul is salvage. Not cruel
  about it. It just mentions it. *You could have had him.*

## 8. Staging the transfer

- **No dice check.** She paid everything she has; a bad roll after that would be miserable. The payment
  *is* the mechanic.
- It's far beyond her level. It should visibly cost her to hold.
- **Angelique feels it happen.** She's elsewhere in the house and her head comes up — the first time in
  five years the pull has *moved.* She comes running with no idea why, and now she's in the room.

## 9. The band thread and the payoff

- **Shared fandom is a rivalry, not a bond.** Poe Boy loves the same band. She's the real one and the
  coolest kid in school likes what she likes, and the school lumps them together as the two dark ones.
  The session is proof they're opposites: she worships, he cosplays.
- **The ticket asymmetry:** she sold spells for eight months to afford one. He didn't pay for anything.
- **The collapse, extending Sesh 8's driveway beat:** Volvo, *"Upton, honey?"* — **"You're grounded.
  And you are certainly not going to that satan concert."**
- **Then his mother notices Simone's band shirt** — the same shirt the school has mocked for four years —
  says something warm and completely clueless, and **gives her the tickets.** Somebody should use them.
- **Upgrades worth taking:** they're better than what she lost (the guest-list passes her money could
  never have bought, now hers and not his), and **there are extras**, so the loner has to decide who to
  bring. That's the actual last beat of the arc, not the ticket.
- **⚠️ The money does not come back.** She's broke. She got the show and not the savings, and she didn't
  earn the tickets — somebody's mom handed them to her. Grace isn't payment, and it arrives from the
  least cool possible source.

## 10. Epilogue

- She goes to the show **with the drive in her pocket.** He's there.
- Optional and very campy: **Dracula comes.** He has opinions about the opener.
- **Her rate card gets a new line:** *All love spells expire in 30 days.* No refunds.
- **Optional last knife:** she's the only one who can take the spell off Angelique. Lift it and Angelique
  stops noticing — the pull stops, she gets her life back, and she forgets a boy she never really knew.
  That's either the kindest thing anyone does all weekend or the last erasure that kid suffers, and
  Simone doesn't get told which. The version where she *doesn't* lift it, and Angelique keeps noticing on
  purpose, is Angelique choosing to carry him — the only real amends available to her.
