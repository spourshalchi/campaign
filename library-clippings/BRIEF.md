## The campaign in four sentences

Campy early-2000s young-adult mystery, played as D&D. A group of early-to-mid-20s super
seniors in their final year of fantasy high school in **Transyltown, NY** — a charming
Hudson Valley suburb. They get grouped together for a **history class project on local
folklore**, then all find a mysterious invitation in their lockers to a haunted house at
the abandoned Dutch mansion on the edge of town. Two sessions, Saturday and Sunday: roughly
the first third to half of session one happens in town, everything after that is the mansion.

**The twist:** the three vampires in the mansion are not the villains. The person who lured
them in is **DJ Poe Boy**, a goth teen DJ scoring a horror movie who wants to sample the
screams of real beings terrorized by real monsters.

## What this handout is

The party's in-town research stop, before the mansion. Sesh 1 called it "a library or hall
of records type deal." It is one of three named printed handouts, alongside the **Haunted
House Invite** and the **DJ Show Flyer**.

It has **two jobs**:

1. **Deliver the lore** the party needs — including, on one branch, the method for killing
   a vampire.
2. **Deliver the clincher.** Sesh 1: "the clincher for them definitely needing to go to the
   mansion will be delivered here."

The **librarian / records keeper is the harbinger** — the one NPC who warns them off going
to the mansion. Spencer wanted "some sort of modern spin on the archetype." That character
is not written yet.

## Source material (all of it already researched, in the doc)

### 1. The fampier — the load-bearing clue

Dutch folklore has almost no vampires. The one real find, from a Frisian folklore site:

> **Vampire (Fampier) (Friesland):** Vampires do not typically feature in Dutch folklore;
> nevertheless, in 1973, the Frisian skipper Steven de Bruin recounted a legend concerning
> children who were found dead on the heath, bearing the imprint of teeth upon their throats.
> A "devil-banisher" discovered a fresh corpse buried in the ground at that very spot. He
> declared: "This is the evil that has been found upon the heath." He placed the body inside
> a new coffin, positioning a wooden splinter within the lid so that it pointed directly at
> the corpse's heart. As they nailed the coffin shut, they heard a terrible howling and
> wailing emanating from within. The corpse was buried once more and could do no further
> harm. (See also the *champier* and the *bloedsûger*.) — Poortinga, *Skaakspul* 258

Spencer's read: this gives **the method for defeating them** — dig up the coffins holding
the vampires' bodies, stake through the heart, rebury.

⚠️ **Sesh 9 cut the fork**, so this is no longer the payoff of a branch. Its job now is to send the
party into the mansion believing they will have to kill three vampires — which makes the reveal land
harder, because they walk in armed with a method and walk out having never needed it.

### 2. The Frisian Kingdom — the setting/period texture

Spencer: "the setting for where the stories take place… we can reference it for making
materials/interactives."

- The ancient **Frisii** lived in the low country between the Zuiderzee and the River Ems.
- Pre-Migration Period (before ~AD 300), the Frisii and the related Chauci, Saxons and Angles
  held the continental coast from the Zuyder Zee to south Jutland. Shared material culture,
  so they can't be told apart archaeologically.
- It was long assumed the Frisii abandoned the land and vanished from the record by AD 400 —
  but recent excavations in the dunes of **Kennemerland** show continuous habitation.
- **Malorix and Verritus**, Frisii chieftains, 1st century AD: in **58 AD** they asked the
  Roman emperor **Nero** to cede territory. While waiting, they walked into the **Theatre of
  Pompey** and sat down among the **Roman senators** without being given permission. Nero
  granted them Roman citizenship — and gave the Frisian territory to Rome's allies anyway.

Malorix and Verritus are the most usable thing here: two named historical figures who showed
up somewhere they weren't invited and sat down anyway. That rhymes with the party walking
into the mansion.

### 3. The Gjenganger — the mansion's other haunt (STILL AN OPEN QUESTION)

Scandinavian revenant. Spencer proposed it and ended with "Up to you if we go with it."

> A Gjenganger is a ghost revived from death to purposefully haunt the living. The word
> combines "again" and "ganger" (walker) — "walking again." They look wholly corporeal, not
> at all ghostly. Typically those who died before their time: murder, suicide, another crime
> or accident. Revenge-motivated and extremely vicious. They attack in the dead of night
> while the victim sleeps, pinching them and fleeing; the victim wakes to a strange blue
> spot, a sign of coming sickness and death. They also spread sickness generally — if it
> touches you, even a brush, your flesh begins to slough away as the virus reaches your heart.
> **To prevent one, a person at risk must be buried in a specific way, with a specific runic
> inscription written inside the coffin** so they cannot wake and walk.

Spencer's proposed backstory: the Gjenganger is the **original owner of the mansion** — never
married, no children, isolated himself in the house and eventually killed himself out of
loneliness. That is a big part of why the house was abandoned, separate from the vampire
rumors. It also gives Will's antisocial character a development beat.

Combat plan: it has the advantage at close range, so the counterplay is distance. It guards
the room with the haunted computer.

## Decisions to make before drawing anything

1. **Library or hall of records?** Never resolved. Newspaper clippings and property deeds
   look nothing alike and imply different NPCs.
2. **How explicit is the staking method?** It is the fight-branch payoff, so how plainly the
   fampier story spells out "splinter, heart, rebury" sets that whole branch's difficulty.
3. **Is the Gjenganger in?** If yes, the runic-inscription burial rite belongs here as a
   second, quieter method — and foreshadows the fight guarding the computer.
4. **Print or prop?** Sesh 7 says print. But the repo builds interactive iPad props, and a
   microfiche reader or a card catalog terminal is a strong retro UI metaphor if it earns it.
