# Torn Page — The Transyltown Crier

> Every name, date and rumour in the 1937 paper is this prop's invention. It leans on: Cornelis Dood (= Orlok) built the house and lived alone in forty rooms; Columbia County.

The party's in-town research stop. A torn front page from the library's bound
volumes: assemble it against the clock, then flip it to read page 2.

**The fiction:** the volume is **intact** when the records keeper hands it over.
It comes apart in front of them.

Opening it springs a trap. The reading room's stationery gets up — loose pages,
desk objects, and a pair of **archival shears** that ignore the party entirely and
go straight for the article. By the time the party puts the shears down, the page
is in pieces, and the pieces are still moving.

**There is no timer.** There used to be one, back when the fiction was a ward
actively fighting them — but the shears are dead and the magic died with them, so
nothing is resisting. It's a jigsaw of a newspaper somebody cut up thirty seconds
ago. The tension already happened; this is the aftermath.

What replaces it is the **difficulty they earned in the fight**, set on a screen
before you hand the iPad over.

Keep this copy in plain present-day language. The 1937 article is written in period
voice on purpose, but the splash and fail screens are the players speaking as
themselves at a D&D table.

The prop never names who set the trap, on purpose, and should stay that way.

> ### 🔔 This prop now has a fight in front of it, and it summons the villain
>
> **The trap is the developer's.** He is a **wizard, an abjurer** — he didn't hire anyone, he set it
> himself, which is why there's no contractor for the party to trace. He is the only character who
> dies in the campaign.
>
> **Springing it tells him someone has been in his records, and he drives out to the house that
> night.** The party's cleverest moment in the town half is the reason the worst thing in the
> building arrives at 3AM, and it is entirely their doing. They will not find out until it happens,
> and ideally not until afterwards.
>
> The trap **contains and erases; it does not kill.** He is a businessman scrubbing a stigmatised
> property, not a murderer, and he would not leave something lethal in a public library where
> children read. That matters — it's what makes him frightening eight hours later, when he *is*
> willing.

**For the DM: the trap is the real-estate developer's.** Not to hide monsters — to
erase a documented 1937 vampire incident from the public record before he develops
the site. A stigmatized property is a liability, so he made the article
unrecoverable rather than argue with it. He can walk into a records hall in
daylight, which the vampires cannot, and he almost certainly doesn't believe a word
of what he destroyed.

None of that is in the prop. The players just watch a book try to eat itself.

## The encounter

Five level-3 PCs. Budget is **750 low / 1,125 moderate / 2,000 high**; this sits at
**500 XP**, deliberately low — it is three rounds of chaos, not a set piece.

| What | Reskin of | CR | XP | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Archival shears** ×2 | Flying Sword | ½ | 100 ea | The two with the mission |
| **Swarm of index cards** ×1 | Swarm of Ravens | ½ | 100 | Blinds and obscures so nobody can see the desk |
| **The book cart** ×1 | Animated Armor | 1 | 200 | Rolls, rams, pins people against the shelves |

**500 XP · 2.5 total CR.** The shears are the only cutting things in the room on
purpose — nothing else can damage the page, so it's never ambiguous which enemies
matter.

That's below the 750 "low" line, and that's fine: **the party will spend most of
their actions body-blocking rather than attacking**, so it plays harder than the
number reads. If you want more pressure, a second swarm takes it to 600 without
changing anything else.

**The shears do not attack people.** Every turn they move for the article and cut.
They only hurt a PC who physically interposes. A weapon that ignores you is much
worse than one that doesn't, and it tells the party what the objective is without
anyone saying it.

**A cut only lands if a pair of shears reaches the page unobstructed.** That is
the whole encounter: two shears, two approach lanes, and a party that has to cover
both with bodies. Killing one halves the pressure immediately, which gives them a
clear target priority without you naming it.

**The first cut is the trigger, not an action.** The shears come out of the spine
as the trap fires — before anybody rolls initiative. That guarantees the page
always gets cut at least once, with no fudging and no special rules. Then roll.

Flying Sword's AC 17 means a level-3 party will miss a lot, which does the rest of
the work.

**Count the cuts. That's the puzzle.** The prop opens on a **setup screen** —
easy / medium / hard — and you set it by how many cuts the shears got in. Then
hand the iPad over. No config editing at the table.

| Cuts landed | Setting | Pieces |
|---|---|---|
| One or two | Easy | 9 |
| Three or four | Medium | 16 |
| Five or more | Hard | 25 |

They can't save the page — otherwise there's no prop — but how badly it goes is
theirs. It turns a fight they're structurally going to lose into one where the
outcome is in their hands.

## The checkout card

In the pocket at the back of the volume, viewable from the splash screen. It shows
the book sat untouched for decades, and then three borrowers:

- **E. Hoeck**, 1938 — texture: kin of Pieter Hoeck, whose beagle came home splinted
  (page 2). Somebody else reading about this house in 1938. Not a blood tie to any PC.
- **V. Soprano**, Sep 1938 — **Gnomey's great-uncle Vinny**, who has owned the
  mansion since he won it off Orlok at cards in 1931 and is reading up on what he
  took. This is the library hint Spencer asked for, and the only place the name
  appears before the study.
- **U. M. Livingsworth III**, Mar 2004 — **Poe Boy's real name.** Nobody at the table
  knows that. It pays off at sunrise when a woman in a fleece gets out of a Volvo and
  says *"Upton, honey?"* — and in the meantime it quietly says he was interested
  in this house before the party.
- **W. Ostrander**, Jul 2004 — the developer.

It also does structural work: it proves **two other people got here first**, and the
party has met neither of them.

Edit the names in the `#ccard` table in `index.html`. R. Ackerman is texture.

Front page is a rendered image (it gets torn into pieces). Page 2 is live HTML
(it has to stay legible at table distance) — so the lore copy is editable here
without regenerating any art.

## What the paper says

A Halloween-week local issue, Thursday 28 October 1937. It gives the house a reputation
and a few rumours. Each one is true or harmlessly wrong and echoes something later in
the house; none is explained on the page.

| Where | What | Echoes |
|---|---|---|
| Front, lead | **"The Dood House Answers Its Bell."** Three boys ring the bell on the old dare; the door opens with no lamp lit and a polite voice asks them in. A tall man in evening clothes, or only a voice from deep in the house, depending on the boy. None goes in | the vampires' hospitality, Dracula; Gnomey's crew and the doorbell (Sesh 5) |
| Front | The Sheriff gets no answer by daylight, as every caller has; the house is private but he can't say whose | the developer's daylight knocks; the settlement and the deed |
| Front | The late Cornelis Dood built it and lived alone with forty rooms; it is kept up and nobody is paid to keep it | Orlok |
| Page 2 | The hill road's talk: no shutter opened by day, one lit window, gramophone music and a party on summer nights, trucks up the hill after midnight in the dry years | the sun; the party room; Vinny and the cellar |
| Page 2 | A beagle home with its broken leg splinted in clean linen; hens with two small marks that go on laying | Orlok's clinic |
| Page 2 | **"Van Wyck's Oldest Account":** a standing order left at the gate four times a year (lamp oil, candles, nails, putty, white lead, carbolic soap), paid from a tin on the gatepost, partly in old silver; nobody ever seen | the kept-up house; Orlok's clinic |
| Page 2 | Four small ads, one of them the lost hat | texture |

Read aloud, page 2 runs about a minute and a half. The front's body text is too small to read
once assembled; the headline, deks and photo caption carry it.

## Editing the copy

**Page 2** — edit the `<template id="article-heath">` block in `index.html`.
Plain HTML. Classes: `.sub` small-caps subhead, `.q` indented quote, `.big`
boxed pull quote, `.box` sidebar, `.drop` drop cap, `.cut` halftone
illustration with caption. No re-render needed.

The sheet gets a torn silhouette and the front page's tear is regenerated on
every deal, so no two runs rip the same way.

**Front page** — edit `assets/front-heath.source.html`, then:

```bash
./assets/render-front.sh
```

Renders at 1860×2622 through headless Chrome and converts to JPEG (the halftone
screen is high-frequency detail that PNG can't compress — 5MB vs 1.2MB, which
matters for first load on the iPad over LAN). Fonts are baked in at render
time, so nothing needs to exist on the iPad.

Two rules when editing the front page:

1. **Keep "Continued on Page 2" visible.** It's the in-fiction prompt to flip.
   If you add copy, cut copy elsewhere. The columns are tuned to fill the page
   exactly — check the bottom of column 2 after any change.
2. **Keep the page full of ink.** Blank regions make torn pieces
   indistinguishable and the puzzle frustrating.

## Adding a second clipping

1. Copy `assets/front-heath.source.html`, edit, render to a new filename
2. Add a `<template id="article-yourname">` next to the existing one
3. Add an entry to `CLIPPINGS` in `index.html`
4. Point `ACTIVE` at it

Candidate already floated: the real-estate firm scouting the mansion.

## Tuning

All in `CONFIG` at the top of the script block.

- `TILE_COUNT: 4` → 16 pieces. **Default only** — the setup screen overrides it
  at the table.
- `EDGE_SEGMENTS: 13` / `EDGE_JITTER_FRAC` — how ragged the rips are. The seam
  is a correlated random walk, not per-point noise: raise the jitter much past
  ~0.13 and the tears stop looking like paper and start looking like starbursts.
- `OUTER_JITTER_FRAC` — the outside of the page is torn too, so the assembled
  sheet isn't a clean rectangle. Outer seams only bite **inward**; a piece
  can't paint outside its own box, so outward jitter would just flat-cut.
- `FRAME_MAX_H_PX: 680` — the page is portrait, so **height** is the binding
  constraint. Don't cap width; that renders it tiny on a landscape iPad.
- `READER_AUTO_MS: 620` — delay before page 2 opens itself after the flip.

Long-press the piece counter for 1.2s to open the debug panel (solve,
re-scatter).
