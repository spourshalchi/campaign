Vol. LXVI  ·  No. 43 Transyltown, N.Y., Thursday, October 28, 1937 Five Cents

THIRD CHILD FOUND
ON THE HEATH

Sleeping, Cold, and Marked at the Throat;
Doctors Cannot Wake Them
Sheriff Sets Sundown Curfew — Village Sends to Kinderhook
for a “Devil‑Banisher”
The Dood house on Vlietberg Road, closed since 1904, photographed Tuesday night from the low ground where the child was found. Searchers are visible at the fence line.

Aletta Bruin, 11, a gnome, of Vlietberg Road, was found before dawn Tuesday in the low heath below the old Dood property — the third child taken from this village since Michaelmas, and the third to be carried home breathing and unwakeable.

She was discovered by Mr. Hendrik Ruys, who had gone out early after a strayed heifer and who at first took the child for a bundle of clothing left in the grass.

“She was cold as the ground,” Mr. Ruys told this paper. “But she was warm at the mouth. That is the part I cannot put down.”

Two Before Her

The Hoeck boy was found on the eleventh of the month, and the Verhoeven girl on the sixteenth, both within a quarter mile of the same fence line and both in the same condition.

In each case the mark is the same: two punctures upon the left side of the throat, no wider than a pin and ringed in white. Dr. Vance has photographed them and declines to publish the plate.

None of the three is known to have gone out. The Bruin child was put to bed at eight o’clock, and the window of her room was found fastened from the inside.

Sheriff Corwin Stade has ordered every child of the village kept indoors from sundown until full light, and has posted two men at the Vlietberg turn with instructions to stop any person going up toward the house after dark.

The Dood house itself has been searched twice this month and found empty. Its doors are chained, its lower windows boarded from within, and the seals set by the trustees in 1904 were reported unbroken.

Doctors Baffled

Dr. Emmerich Vance, who has attended all three children, has declined to name the condition, saying only that he has read of nothing resembling it in thirty years of practice.

“They are not asleep. Sleep has a bottom to it. This does not.”

The Fields Also

Sheep belonging to Mr. Ruys and to the Dekker farm have been found dead in these same fields since September, in a manner the Sheriff has asked this paper not to set beside the other matter in print.

This paper has set them side by side, and will answer for it.

The last family to keep the house were the Doods, of whom none remain in the county. It has passed since 1904 through three trusts, the last giving its address as a post office box in Albany.

At a meeting of the trustees held Wednesday evening in the Reformed church, it was voted, with two abstaining, to send to Kinderhook for a man named Joost Wiegman.

Mr. Wiegman is described by those who called for him as a duivelbanner — a banisher of devils — an office his family has kept in this valley since before the patents.

The measure was opposed by the Rev. Mr. Bloem, who warned that the village would be laughed at in Poughkeepsie.

“Let them laugh,” said Mrs. Ilse Verhoeven, whose daughter has not opened her eyes since the sixteenth. “I would be laughed at every day of my life.”

His Conditions

Mr. Wiegman is expected before first light. He has sent ahead two conditions, and the trustees have agreed to both.

The first is that no person follow him onto the heath, whatever should be heard from it.

The second is that a new coffin be built of good boards and carried down to the fence line, and left there open and unnailed.

He did not say what it was for.
